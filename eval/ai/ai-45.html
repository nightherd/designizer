What the first hour looks like

0:00
Sand, not water We learn the pop-up on the beach, board flat under your hands, until it feels boring. That is the goal.
0:20
Knee-deep, then waist-deep We walk out together and catch whitewater. Your instructor stays next to you, not ahead.
0:50
Your first ride A small wave, a clear call from the shore, and a few seconds standing. Most people get up before the first session ends.
1:30
Read the sea We look at the set, the rip and where the wave breaks, so the next paddle goes somewhere useful.

Ribeira d'Ilhas, Ericeira

Ericeira is a fishing town with a surf culture older than most of its surf shops. The reef breaks and sandy beaches here take a swell from the Atlantic, so the same stretch of coast can hold a gentle lesson in the morning and a proper barrel by afternoon.

Our school is a ten-minute walk from the old town, past the fish market. We keep the boards, wetsuits and warm showers on site. Lisbon is about forty minutes away by car, and the airport is closer than you think.

Taught by people who still paddle out on their days off.

Our instructors are local surfers, trained in water safety and first aid, and most of them learned on these same beaches. Lessons stay small: no more than four students per instructor, so you get corrected and not just watched.
