Our mission

Seeds belong to everyone who grows them.

Commercial seed catalogs have narrowed the varieties we can buy. We keep local, open-pollinated varieties in circulation, adapted to our climate, and passed from garden to garden.

1

Open-pollinated only

Every variety in our library can be saved and replanted true to type. No hybrids, no restrictions.

2

Adapted to here

Seeds that have grown in our soil and weather for generations do better than anything shipped from afar.

3

Run by members

The co-op is governed by its members. Volunteers catalog, test, grow out and host every workshop.

How it works

Borrow, grow, return.

Think of it like a library card, except the book comes back with a few new chapters.

  1. 1
    Check out a packetPick up to three varieties per season at a branch or during Saturday hours. Every packet is free to members.
  2. 2
    Grow and saveLet a few plants go to seed, dry them, and label the harvest with the variety name and year.
  3. 3
    Return a shareBring back a portion of your seed by the end of the season. Your return keeps the shelves full for everyone.

Do I need experience to join?

No. Many of our best seed savers started with a single tomato plant on a balcony. Workshops cover the basics, and experienced members are always glad to help.

What if my harvest fails?

It happens. Return what you can, even a small amount, and let us know what went wrong. Failed seasons teach the co-op as much as good ones do.

Are the seeds organic or pesticide-free?

Most of our seed is grown by members without synthetic pesticides. Each packet is labeled with the grower's notes on growing conditions where we have them.

Can I donate seed or money?

Yes to both. Clean, labeled seed from your garden is always welcome at the library. Financial gifts go toward storage, printing and our community plots.
