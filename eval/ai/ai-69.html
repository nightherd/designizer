What we do in the barn

Most of our work happens standing in the aisle with a lead rope in one hand and a clipboard in the other. The list below is what we see most often, and what we bring in the truck for each.

01

Lameness & orthopedics

Nerve blocks, flexion tests, and a lot of patience. We work up the leg from the hoof to the stifle, then use radiographs and ultrasound to find where the problem actually is.

  • Digital radiography, portable on farm
  • Joint injections and shockwave therapy
02

Reproduction & foaling

Cycle management, breeding soundness exams, pregnancy checks by transrectal ultrasound, and foaling attendance for mares who need a second set of hands at 2 a.m.

  • Cooled and frozen semen handling
  • Neonatal foal care and monitoring
03

Dentistry

Sharp enamel points cause more trouble than most owners expect. We float teeth under sedation, check for wolf teeth and diastemas, and note what we changed so the next visit is easier.

  • Power floating with a written dental chart
  • Extractions and sinus-related work
04

Colic & emergencies

Colic is the one call we do not ask you to wait on. We bring pain relief, a nasogastric tube, and a rectal exam to the farm. If the horse needs surgery, we tell you early and drive it to the referral hospital ourselves.

  • Nasogastric intubation and fluid therapy
  • Referral transport to university hospitals
05

Vaccines, Coggins & health papers

Routine care that keeps the herd healthy and the trailer legal. Core vaccines by region and risk, parasite programs built around your pasture, and health certificates for interstate travel and shows.

  • Coggins testing and same-day paperwork
  • Health certificates for travel and competition
06

Imaging & diagnostics

Ultrasound, endoscopy, and bloodwork run in our own lab. Most results come back the same day, so a diagnosis does not have to wait a week for a courier.

  • Standing endoscopy for airway and gut work
  • In-house CBC, chemistry and fecal testing

How a farm call works

No automated phone tree and no surprise fees. You describe what you are seeing, we tell you when we will arrive, and the bill matches what we quoted you.

  1. Step one

    You call

    Describe the horse, what changed and when. A doctor calls back within 15 minutes during office hours, and right away after hours.

  2. Step two

    We triage

    Most calls are settled by phone with a plan. If the horse needs us in person, we give you a window, not a vague “sometime today.”

  3. Step three

    We examine

    The doctor walks the horse, asks about feed, turnout and past history, and explains findings as they come up, before anything is done.

  4. Step four

    You get a plan

    A written summary with what we did, what it costs, what to watch for, and when we will check again.
