Three days to make. Gone by noon.

We bake naturally leavened bread from flour, water, salt, and a fourteen-year-old starter named Gerald. Nothing else goes in the country loaf. It's been that way since we opened.

Tap the bottom of a good loaf and it sounds hollow. That's the name. That's also the test every loaf on our racks passes before it's sold.

Every loaf you buy was started on Monday.

Long, cold fermentation is slow and a little inconvenient. It's also why the bread tastes like something and keeps for days.

Day one

Wake Gerald

  1. 06:00Feed the starter 1:5:5 with rye and white flour.
  2. 14:00Build the levain. It should double by evening.
  3. 17:30Autolyse — flour and water, left alone for an hour.
  4. 18:30Mix in levain and salt, by hand, in big tubs.
Day two

Fold, shape, wait

  1. 19:00Four sets of stretch-and-folds, thirty minutes apart.
  2. 23:00Divide, pre-shape, bench rest.
  3. 23:40Final shape into linen-lined baskets.
  4. 00:00Into the walk-in at 4°C for 14–18 hours.
Day three

Score & bake

  1. 04:30Deck oven up to 250°C. Coffee.
  2. 05:15Turn out, score with a lame, load with steam.
  3. 05:35Vent the steam, drop the heat, bake dark.
  4. 06:40First rack out. Knock. Listen.

Gerald is older than the bakery.

He started in a jam jar on Maren's kitchen counter in 2014 — rye flour, tap water, and a lot of patience. He's been fed twice a day ever since, including one Christmas in a cooler on the back seat of a Volvo.

Every loaf we sell carries a bit of him. If you'd like some for your own kitchen, ask at the counter. It's free. Bring a clean jar.
