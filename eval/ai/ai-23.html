How it works

Three habits. You'll pick them up in a week.

1

Say what's coming in.

Paycheck, freelance invoice, the $40 your brother owes you. Put the income at the top of the page and subtract rent and bills you already know about. What remains is your month.

2

Write things down as they happen.

Open the app, type "lunch 14.50", done. No bank login required, though you can connect one if you'd rather not type. Entries take about four seconds, and we've timed it.

3

Close the page on the 1st.

Kept shows what you spent against what you planned, rolls any surplus forward, and starts a clean sheet. Last month is never a lecture; it's just a page you can flip back to.

On purpose

Things Kept will never do.

Gamify your savingsNo streaks, badges or confetti. Saving $200 is its own reward, and a cartoon owl doesn't need to know.

Sell your dataYour ledger lives on your devices and syncs encrypted. We make money from the price below, and nothing else.

Guess your categoriesTag things how you think about them. "Dana stuff" is a perfectly good category.

Send guilt notificationsOne optional nudge on Sunday evening with your remaining balance. That's the entire notification system.

Questions

Reasonable ones.

Does it connect to my bank?

Optionally. If you link an account, transactions show up as suggestions that you approve with a tap. Nothing gets added behind your back, and you can use Kept without ever connecting anything.

Can my partner use it too?

Not yet. Kept is deliberately built for one person. A shared version is something we're thinking about slowly, rather than something we're promising.

What if I forget to log for a week?

Then you log a week on Sunday. There's no penalty and no broken chain. The page is just a page.

Is there an Android app?

Not at the moment. It's the most common request, and it's on the list after the Mac app gets a few more rounds of polish.
