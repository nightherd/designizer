What an afternoon looks like

Every session follows the same plan, so kids know what's coming and can get straight to building.

Kids work in pairs: one types, one navigates, and they swap halfway. They explain their thinking to each other, which is most of how they learn.

3:30 Snack and a warm-up puzzle Five minutes, no screens. Today: guide a friend through a maze using only arrow cards.
3:45 Today's challenge A short demo, then pairs build their own version. Mentors walk the room.

Two groups, split by age

No experience needed for either group. If your child has already coded a lot, we'll find them a harder challenge.

Ages 7–9: block coders

Kids snap code blocks together in Scratch. They don't need to type, and they need only a little reading.

  • Animate a story with characters that talk and move
  • Build a maze game with a timer and a score
  • Program a small floor robot to deliver a note across the room

Ages 10–12: text coders

Kids type real Python and see what it does right away. They start with turtle drawings and move on to games.

  • Draw patterns and spirals with the Python turtle
  • Make a micro:bit step counter they can wear home
  • Design, build and playtest a game of their own by the end of term

Questions parents ask

My child has never coded. Is that okay?

Yes. Most kids start with no experience. The first two sessions cover the basics, and pairs are matched so nobody gets left behind.

What happens at the free trial session?

Your child joins a normal session, from snack to show-and-tell. You can stay for the first 15 minutes if it helps them settle. There's no obligation to sign up afterwards.

What if we miss a session?

Each challenge stands on its own, so missing one is fine. We email a short recap after each session so kids can try it at home if they want.

Will my child be on screens the whole time?

No. About a third of each session is away from screens: warm-up puzzles, planning on paper and show-and-tell.

Can I pay in instalments?

Yes. You can pay in two parts, at the start and middle of term. If cost is a problem, ask us about a free place. We don't ask for proof.
