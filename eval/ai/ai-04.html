01 — The problem

Your password policy can't help if the password was stolen off someone's home PC.

Infostealer malware grabs saved browser passwords, session cookies and autofill data, then ships it off in a neat folder. Those folders get sold in bulk, often for a few dollars each. Your MFA and rotation rules never get a say.

03 — How it works

About twenty minutes to set up. Then you mostly forget about it.

  1. Verify your domains

    Add a TXT record for each domain you own. Subsidiaries and old acquired domains count too, and that's often where the forgotten accounts are.

  2. Connect your directory (optional)

    Hook up Entra ID, Okta or Google Workspace and we'll check leaked passwords against current hashes. Matching happens locally via our agent, and plaintext never leaves your network.

  3. Get the backlog

    Your first report covers everything already on file, usually a few hundred entries. We sort it by "still works" vs. "already rotated", so you only deal with the first group.

  4. Act on new alerts

    New hits arrive in Slack, Teams, email, or a webhook. Connect your IdP and you can force a reset with one click, or automatically with auto_reset: true.

05 — Ground rules

Things we decided not to do.

Store plaintext passwords

We hash on ingest. Your dashboard shows the first and last two characters, which is enough for a human to recognise the password and nothing more.

Pay criminals for data

No purchases from threat actors and no "buying back" your data. Everything we hold was publicly posted or traded, or collected through lawful research access.

Sell a risk score

A 72/100 doesn't tell you what to do. We give you account names, what was exposed, and the one action we'd take.

Scare you into a demo

The domain check above is real, and so are the counts. If there's nothing to worry about, we'll say so.

07 — Questions

Things people ask on the first call.

How is this different from Have I Been Pwned?

HIBP is great, and we use it ourselves. It focuses on public breaches, though, and works per email address. Most of what we alert on comes from stealer logs, which HIBP mostly doesn't cover. We also check whether the leaked password still works on your directory, so you're not chasing ones that were rotated years ago.

Do you need access to our password hashes?

No. Directory matching runs in a small agent on your network. It receives k-anonymised hash prefixes from us and does the comparison locally. We only ever learn "match" or "no match".

What about customers' accounts, not just employees?

On Programme you can watch any domain you can prove a relationship with, which covers customer login domains too. Most people use this to spot account-takeover risk before it reaches support.

What happens when the infected device isn't ours?

That's the usual case. We tell you what we can (OS, approximate infection date, which other corporate apps were in the log) so you can have a useful conversation with the employee. We don't de-anonymise anyone or do anything to the device.

Where is data stored?

EU (Frankfurt) or US (Virginia), your choice, and fixed at signup. SOC 2 Type II report available on request under NDA.
