The method

Three signals. One honest guess at each stage.

Wrist motion and heart rate are good at telling asleep from awake. They are weaker at separating light sleep from deep sleep. Dimmer shows you that uncertainty instead of hiding it.

  1. 01

    Movement

    An accelerometer notes when you are still and when you turn over. Long stillness after lights-out is the first thing the app looks for.

  2. 02

    Heart rate and its variability

    Heart rate drops during deep sleep, and its beat-to-beat variation rises during REM. Dimmer reads both and draws the stage boundaries from them.

  3. 03

    Your own baseline

    After about ten nights the app stops comparing you to an average person and starts comparing you to you. Stage lengths are scored against your last month.

Questions

Things people ask first.

Is this a medical device?

No. Dimmer is a personal log. It does not diagnose sleep apnea, insomnia or any other condition. If a pattern worries you, bring the export to a clinician. Many of them find it useful.

How accurate are the sleep stages?

Good enough to see trends over weeks, not good enough to read a single night as fact. Asleep versus awake is reliable. Deep versus light is an estimate, and the app labels it that way.

Does it work without a watch?

Yes. A phone on the mattress or nightstand picks up movement and gives you duration and wake counts. You lose heart rate and the stage detail.

Can I use it on rotating shifts?

That is the main case it is built for. You can tag each sleep period as main sleep, a nap or a recovery block, and the weekly view will average them separately so a day-shift nap does not hide a short night.
