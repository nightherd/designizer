So we built the thing around the site, not the trailer.

A Tuesday on site

Not a feature tour. This is where it gets used.

  1. 05:45

    Check the board in the truck

    The foreman sees who’s coming, what got moved overnight, and the weather call. One screen, big buttons, readable in sunlight.

  2. 07:00

    Toolbox talk, signed in

    Crew taps in at the gate. Safety topic is already queued. Sign-ins land in the daily log without anyone typing a name twice.

  3. 09:20

    Found a problem. Snap it.

    Photo, a thumb-drawn circle, a voice note: “beam pocket is 2 inches off.” It’s pinned to the plan and sent to the engineer before the coffee’s cold.

  4. 12:10

    Change order, in writing

    The extra work gets priced on the spot with the photo attached. The owner approves from their phone. You stop eating the cost of “verbal okays.”

  5. 15:30

    Daily log writes itself

    Headcount, hours, weather, deliveries, photos. Review, tap once, done. Not at 9 p.m. from the couch.

What’s in the box

01

Crew scheduling

Drag a crew from one job to another. Conflicts show up in red before they show up as a no-show.Handles subs, day labor, and equipment on the same board.

02

Daily logs

Auto-filled from check-ins, weather, and delivery receipts. Export to PDF the way your GC or insurer expects it.Time-stamped. Can’t be quietly edited later.

03

Plans & markups

Current sheets only. Old revisions get a big “SUPERSEDED” stamp so nobody builds off Rev B.Pinch to zoom on 200-sheet sets without the app choking.

04

Punch lists

Assign to a trade, require a photo to close, and watch the count go down. Nothing gets “done” on a hunch.Try it just below.

05

Works with no signal

Everything you’ve opened that day is stored on the phone. Edits queue up and sync when you’re back in range.Tested in a parking garage, three floors down.

06

Glove mode

Larger targets, high contrast, and voice for notes. Because nobody takes work gloves off to type.Also: no logins that expire halfway up a scaffold.
