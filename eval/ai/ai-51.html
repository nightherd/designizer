Three steps, and most of the work is reading.

You don't write a decision tree. Porter learns what your store already says, then you tell it which actions it may take on its own and which need a person.

1

Point it at your help center

Paste a sitemap or connect your docs. Porter indexes articles, macros and past replies, and shows you the answers it would give to your 50 most common questions before anything goes live.

sync: helpcenter.example.com/sitemap.xml

2

Connect the systems it can look things up in

Read-only access to orders, subscriptions and shipments is enough to answer most questions. Write actions such as cancel, address change or credit are off until you switch them on one at a time.

permissions: orders.read, shipments.read

3

Set the lines it must not cross

Refund thresholds, tone rules, topics that always go to a human. Each rule is a sentence you write, and every conversation records which rule decided the outcome.

escalate_if: refund > 50 OR mentions(legal)

The handoff is the product. Not the chat window.

Most bots fail by pretending. Porter is built to stop early, say what it has done, and leave your agent with less to do than before the customer wrote in.

never guesses money

Refunds, credits and price adjustments are either inside a rule you wrote or they go to a person. Porter never invents a discount to end a conversation.

says what it looked at

Every answer that depends on an order, account or policy shows its source in the agent view. If a source is missing, the reply says so instead of filling the gap.

hands over with context

The agent receives a summary, the lookups already made and the customer's last three messages. Nobody has to ask for an order number twice.

gives up after two tries

If the customer rephrases the same problem twice, Porter stops and offers a person. It will not keep looping on a question it has already failed.

Questions people ask before the demo.

Will customers know they're talking to a bot?

Yes. The chat says so on the first message and every handoff is labelled. Porter does not use a human name or photo unless you configure one for your team, and you can turn that off.

What happens to conversations it can't finish?

They move into your existing inbox with the transcript and any lookups already made. Nothing is dropped and the customer doesn't see a queue message they have to repeat themselves for.

Can it issue refunds on its own?

Only under amounts and conditions you define, and only if you turn the write permission on. The default is off. Every action it takes is logged with the rule that allowed it.

Where does our data go?

Conversation text is stored in the region you choose and is not used to train shared models. You can delete a customer's history in one action, and the deletion is recorded.

How do we know it's working?

The dashboard shows resolved, handed-off and reopened conversations by topic. Anything that was reopened within 48 hours appears in a weekly review list so you can fix the answer, not just the metric.
